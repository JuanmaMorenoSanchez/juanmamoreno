Vodka con fanta.

2017, Watercolor on paper, 29 x 21 cm.

The Chemical Halo

A painter who wants to show bliss has a choice to make about the light. For centuries, the light of ecstasy was divine, a gift from outside, its golden rays descending to confer grace on the chosen. Then came the light of feeling itself, the world saturated with a colour that expressed an inner state, the painter’s own emotion projected onto a landscape. A third option is harder, and perhaps more true to a world of manufactured feeling. That light comes from within the subject, but it is not their soul made visible. The glow comes from the thing they just consumed.

Juanma Moreno Sánchez’s small watercolor from 2017 is called Vodka con fanta. The title is a provocation in its plainness, naming a cheap, unsophisticated drink, the kind of concoction mixed in a dirty glass at the end of a long night. The woman in the painting is held in a moment of uncomplicated pleasure, eyes closed, a slight smile playing on her lips as she sips through a straw. Nothing about the scene asks to be taken seriously. Yet the painting treats her with a strange, almost visionary reverence. A halo blooms behind her head, but it is not the gold of a saint. The halo is a sickly, beautiful stain of magenta and bile-green, the colours of artificial flavouring and cheap club lighting.

This visual contrast is the work’s argument. The light is a chemical reaction rather than a state of grace. The resulting glow is the visual equivalent of the drink itself, a synthetic aura for a synthetic pleasure. The watercolor bleeds and blossoms, the pigments separating in the wet paper to create an effect that feels both gorgeous and toxic, a private aurora borealis powered by sugar and cheap alcohol. This painting belongs to a period of Moreno Sánchez’s work, just before his dive into the monstrous bodies of generative algorithms, where the subject was often the party and its aftermath. In works like "Festival" and the aptly titled "Noche caótica" from the following year, he documents the disconnected, strange, and sometimes luminous voids of contemporary social gatherings. The figures are often isolated, caught in a private trance.

The visual language for this trance has a surprisingly deep history. Moreno Sánchez seems to have reached back over a century to find it, to the work of the French Symbolist Odilon Redon. In works like The Golden Cell, Redon depicts a disembodied head, eyes closed, floating in an abstract, glowing space. Redon’s painting presents an image of pure interiority, a portrait of a soul detached from the material world. Moreno Sánchez takes this apparatus for depicting the spirit and applies it to the most mundane of subjects. He gives the full Redon treatment, the nimbus of inner light, to a girl drinking vodka and Fanta. The machinery of transcendence is repurposed to show a buzz.

The painting offers a precise observation about where we look for transport now, rather than a criticism of its subject. The work is a secular icon. The pleasure is real, but its source is disposable. It finds its place in Moreno Sánchez’s art as a waypoint between two kinds of looking. The painting was made years after his cool, almost sociological observations of public space in paintings like "Transeúntes", and still some years before the explicit, impossible anatomies of a work like "Ángel III". Here, in 2017, the weirdness is still contained within a human skin, as a private and temporary glow. The risk the painting runs is that its effect depends heavily on its title; without that prosaic anchor, one might be tempted to read a more spiritual depth into her expression than the work intends. I cannot be entirely sure if the face, on its own, escapes a certain generic bliss.

What lingers is not the figure so much as the atmosphere she generates. The background is a marvel of controlled chaos, the dark green wash contaminated by black specks and the violent bloom of the halo. The effect is a picture of a mood physically staining the world around it. The painting does not judge the feeling or the person feeling it. It just watches as the chemistry of a moment works its way outwards, a small, private pleasure leaving a beautiful, toxic residue in its wake.
