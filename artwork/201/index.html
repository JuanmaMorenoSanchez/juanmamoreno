Electricidad estática I.

2026, Oil on canvas on cardboard, 35,56 x 27,94 cm.

Leonardo filled entire notebooks comparing curls to river eddies, but when it came to putting them on canvas, he still smoothed them into continuous waves. Julio Romero de Torres preferred architecture. In Viva el pelo (1928), painted near the end of his life and held at the Museo Julio Romero de Torres in Córdoba, the back of the woman's head becomes a sculpture of oiled, jet-black silk. Held by a small red comb, the hair is an immovable monument to modesty and local myth, set so firmly that the air around it cannot even stir.

Fifteen years ago, Moreno Sánchez tested that same stillness from Córdoba. Canvases like Estudio de Julio Romero de Torres I and the tight braid in Trenza de Patri treated hair as an obedient mass, cut off from the surrounding space and anchored to the spine. Here we are offered the exact opposite. Along the bottom edge, you can barely tell there is a young girl underneath. The figure has been sacrificed so that unruly hair can take over the surface, stripping the portrait of psychology. What used to be an ornament of regional identity has turned into a tangle unraveling in a draft.
