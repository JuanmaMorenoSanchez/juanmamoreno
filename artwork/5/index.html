Partido de beisbol triturado.

2020, Oil on canvas, 114 x 114 cm.

The Team Has Been Deleted

The year the stadiums fell silent was 2020. The sound of a crowd, that vast and simple animal roar, vanished from the air. We watched games played in empty arenas, the choreographed movements of athletes unfolding in an enormous quiet, punctuated only by the thud of a ball and the lonely shouts of players. It was a global ghost season, a spectacle of absence. It is in the context of this profound suspension that Juanma Moreno Sánchez painted Partido de beisbol triturado (Shredded Baseball Game). The title announces a violence done not just to the players but to the very idea of the game itself. There is no green diamond here, no elegant geometry of bases, no simple narrative of innings and scores. The painting gives us the game after it has been fed through a machine that understands nothing of rules or sportsmanship, only data. What is left is the wreckage.

The central figure is turned inside out.

The painting’s method is its meaning. We know Moreno Sánchez uses generative artificial intelligence as a collaborator, feeding it images and using the strange, often broken forms it produces as source material. Think of this process as a digital version of what Hito Steyerl called the “poor image,” an image that has been copied, compressed, circulated, and degraded until it loses its original integrity and gains a new, spectral life. The AI chews through thousands of images of baseball—the pitcher’s wind-up, the batter’s swing, the slide into home—and spits out this anatomical chaos. Moreno Sánchez then takes this digital slurry and renders it with the patient, physical permanence of oil paint. The forms are nonsensical, a pile-up of limbs and what might be viscera under a sky of impossibly serene, synthetic blue, but the paint itself is handled with care. The result is a high-fidelity image of a low-fidelity process, a beautiful painting of a system breaking down.

One of the work-in-progress photographs of this canvas reveals that its foundation is a layer of shocking, electric green. This is not the living green of a mown field. It is the flat, absolute green of a digital green screen, the colour of a space waiting to be filled with a fabricated reality. Traces of this foundation survive at the edges of the figures, a vibrant, unnatural halo that outlines the bodies and gives the whole scene a low electronic hum. That the painting is built upon this colour is everything. The field is not a place; it is a void. This formal choice is a risk. The composition is so relentlessly chaotic, a frantic pile of interlocking forms, that it constantly threatens to become illegible. The painting is saved from collapsing into pure abstraction only by the rigid vertical posts the figures are tangled on and by that placid, unbothered sky.

This heap of broken bodies calls to mind another historical catastrophe, Théodore Géricault’s The Raft of the Medusa. Géricault’s 1819 masterpiece also gives us a pyramid of suffering, a mass of humanity abandoned to its fate. But on that raft, there is a clear vector of hope: at the apex of the pyramid, a man waves a cloth at a distant, barely visible ship. There is a chance of rescue. Moreno Sánchez’s canvas offers no such exit. His figures are not climbing toward salvation but are impaled, hung up for display. In the same year, his diptych Asunción y descenso explored a more orderly, vertical movement of bodies through space, one with clear religious and art-historical echoes. Here, the arrangement is a horizontal horror.

I do not know what to call the wooden structures that pin the scene together. They could be rustic crosses, the architecture of a mass crucifixion. They might be the supports for baseball’s own perimeter fence, now repurposed into something more sinister. The uncertainty feels deliberate. They are frames for displaying broken things. The title promises a baseball game, an activity defined by its central object, the ball itself. But the ball is nowhere to be found. The game has not just been shredded. It has been deleted, leaving behind only the ruins of its players, tangled in the machine that devoured them, cooking under a perfect, empty sky.
