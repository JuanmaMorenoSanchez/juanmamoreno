Ángel I.

2024, Oil on wood, 30 x 30 cm.

The Angel in the Workshop

A painter’s studio is a library of lies. The props gathered there are tools for building a world, and they only tell the truth about their own fakery: the plaster cast that stands in for a Roman hero, the bolt of silk that will be painted as a queen’s gown, the plastic skull that waits to play its part in a lecture on mortality. An angel’s wing, in this context, is not an instrument of flight but a problem of weight, texture, and light. It is an object to be rendered, a beautiful piece of stagecraft whose holiness is a quality to be constructed, not assumed. The work happens in the space between the magnificent name and the mundane thing.

This is the space Juanma Moreno Sánchez occupies in Ángel I. The painting gives us the components of a celestial being laid out with the frankness of a mechanic’s workbench. The title promises divinity, but the canvas delivers an inventory. Here is a wing, plain and white, leaning against a body as if waiting to be attached. Here is a torso, rendered with the anonymous, functional smoothness of a mannequin. Here is a swath of brilliant blue fabric, crumpled on the floor like a dropped costume. The figure’s arms are raised in a gesture that could be ecstasy or surrender, but without a face, it is just a posture. The painting is an anatomy of an icon, a systematic dismantling of a sacred image into its constituent, and very earthly, parts.

This analytical coolness marks a shift in his work. A decade earlier, in a small watercolor like “Paisaje con ángeles” (2010), the divine arrived by stealth, a halo hinted at in a public park. The angelic was a possibility you could almost miss. More recently, in paintings sourced from generative systems like “Catástrofe en el bosque” (2024), the supernatural erupts as a bodily horror, a monstrous fusion of flesh and landscape. Ángel I, along with its companions in the series, proposes a third way. It neither suggests nor deforms. It dissects. The strangeness comes not from a glitch in the code but from the calm, procedural way a being of pure spirit is treated as a thing to be assembled from props. This act of deconstruction has long shadows in his work, visible in the allegorical pile-up of “Asunción” (2015), but here the method is quieter and more direct.

The painting argues with a history much older than the studio. It feels like an inversion of the ex-voto, the votive offerings traditionally left in Spanish churches. Those small, often crudely made paintings or metal plaques depict a single body part, a leg or an eye, offered up in thanks for a miracle. They are fragments of the profane given to the sacred. Moreno Sánchez gives us the sacred in fragments. The wing, the body, the drapery: they are like holy relics stripped of their story, presented not for veneration but for inspection. The tradition of the Spanish ex-voto is one of desperate, focused faith; this painting is an act of cool, curious cataloguing.

For all its analytical distance, the work is not without its pleasures. The painterly delight in the rich crimson and the electric, Yves Klein blue of the fabrics is palpable, a passage of handling so lovely it risks tipping the whole arrangement into something merely decorative. The solution feels, for a moment, almost too neat. What saves the painting from its own beauty is the bottle. It is a stubborn, grubby, resolutely mundane object. Its glass is clouded, its surface scrawled with errant marks of yellow and pink, and it is stoppered with a rough piece of wood whose purpose I cannot guess. The bottle belongs to the world of thirst and waste, not theology.

It is the most honest object in the room. This empty container is the anchor that holds the assembled angel parts to the ground, a reminder of the prosaic world from which all myth-making must begin. The painting may be titled for the angel, for the promise of flight and divine presence, but its truth resides in the weight of this bottle. The work shows us that even an angel, in the end, has to be built from the materials at hand, from the beautiful lies and the one grubby fact, sitting together in the quiet of the workshop.
