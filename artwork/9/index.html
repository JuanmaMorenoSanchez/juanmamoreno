Monstruo del espacio latente (I).

2020, Oil on canvas, 50 x 64,5 cm.

The Portrait of a Ghost

A painter has always had a source for a body. It might be a person paid to sit in the studio, a memory of a face seen on a train, a figure borrowed from another painting, or the uncertain record of a photograph. The source provides a set of problems to be solved in paint. But what is the painter’s work when the source is not a thing in the world, but a statistical ghost, a body dreamed up in the non-place that he gives a name to in the title: the latent space? What does a painter owe to a model that was never alive?

Juanma Moreno Sánchez’s Monstruo del espacio latente (I) is a portrait of just such a ghost. The title is a technical confession, telling us the creature’s origin lies in his own experiments with a generative algorithm rather than in mythology or nightmare. Yet the painting’s work is not technical. The work is one of quiet, tender observation. The creature is not a monster to be slain or a specimen to be dissected. The figure is a sitter. The folds of skin around its pug-like face are rendered with the same patient attention a painter would give to a paying client. Its gaze is heavy, ancient, and weary. It has been given the full dignity of a body, complete with a single human foot and a strange harness that seems to be part life-jacket, part anatomical support. A series of similar works from the same year, like Monstruo del espacio latente (II), shows this was not a one-off encounter but a sustained period of study.

The act of inventing hybrid creatures is not new. In the margins of the 14th-century Luttrell Psalter, anonymous artists drew a riot of grotesques: men with animal bodies, bishops with bird heads, strange beasts cobbled together from parts. Those figures, however, were an energetic, often humorous overflow at the edges of a sacred text. They are a form of play. The creature in Moreno Sánchez’s painting is not playing. The creature is burdened by its own impossible form. It sits on a desolate shore, contemplating a placid sea under a vacant sky, a fully realised being dropped into a world it was not made for. The painting is a portrait of a profound solitude.

This painting from 2020 marks a deep shift in his work. The unease in his earlier canvases, like the social observation of anonymous figures in Transeúntes (2008), was located in the space between people. Here, and in the works that follow, the unease has moved inside the body itself. The figure is no longer just awkward in the world; the world has become an awkwardness in the figure, its form shredded and reassembled, as in the later painting Jardín triturado (2022). Monstruo del espacio latente (I) is a quiet arrival point for this new kind of being, a figure whose very anatomy is the site of the uncanny.

The painting’s greatest risk is that its title over-explains, turning the canvas into a simple demonstration of a technological process. The work survives this because of what the paint does. The pathos in the creature's posture and the melancholy of its gaze are entirely the work of the painter’s hand, not the machine’s suggestion. There is a story here that the algorithm could not tell. I cannot name the small, heart-shaped red object that smolders near its mouth (a strange flower, a cigarette, a valve) and this mystery feels essential. It is a detail that belongs to the creature, not to its source code.

The result is an act of profound, almost severe generosity. The painter has taken a digital phantom, a being with no history and no place, and given it both. He has given it the weight of oil paint, the warmth of flesh, a beach to sit on, and a horizon to contemplate. The painting is the creature's only home, the sole evidence that it was ever here at all.
